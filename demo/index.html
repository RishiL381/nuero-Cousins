Your brain teaches your watch and webcam what stress looks like.

An EEG headband (Muse 2) is precise but nobody wears one all day. Clutch uses it once: in a 9-minute calibration, your brain trains a personal model on your heart rate and face. After that, a watch and a webcam are enough. Below, a real 19-minute recording is replayed second by second through the app's own code.

The takeaway: brain signals generalise across people. Heart and face only work once they are calibrated on the person, and the headset is what calibrates them.

How it works

  1. Calibrate, 9 minutes. Rest, a timed mental-math stress test, recovery. Muse 2 EEG, a Garmin watch and the webcam record together.
  2. The teacher. On each electrode, theta, alpha and beta power in bands tuned to your alpha frequency. Windows with bad contact, head movement or muscle tension are dropped. The signals that change under stress are kept, in whichever direction they move for you.
  3. The student. A small linear model learns the weights that make your heart rate and facial tension reproduce the teacher's curve, second by second.
  4. Take the headset off. The watch and webcam carry on alone.

A pilot: 2 people, 4 sessions, about 34 minutes of labelled data, always tested on unseen data or an unseen person. A wellness prototype, not a medical device. The recording shown is a team member's, shared with consent.
